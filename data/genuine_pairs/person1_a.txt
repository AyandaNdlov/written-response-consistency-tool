The time I had to show leadership was during my vac work at a logistics company in Durban. I was placed in a small team that was meant to analyse why deliveries to one region kept arriving late. Nobody was officially in charge, so for the first week we just did our own thing and nothing really moved.

I suggested that we meet every morning for fifteen minutes. At first people were a bit annoyed because it felt like another meeting, but it helped us see who was doing what. I made a simple spreadsheet where we tracked each late delivery and the reason the driver gave. I'm not an expert in Excel but it was enough.

After two weeks we noticed that most of the delays happened on one route where the depot opened later than the others. That was not something any of us would have found alone. We presented it to our supervisor and she organised a meeting with the depot manager.

What I learnt is that leadership doesn't always mean telling people what to do. Sometimes it is just making sure everyone can see the same information. I also learnt that I need to listen more, because one of the quieter people in the team actually noticed the depot thing first and I nearly talked over her.
