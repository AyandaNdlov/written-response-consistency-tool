Good customer service starts with listening. At the pharmacy where I work, customers are often worried or in pain. They do not always explain things clearly. My first job is to calm them down and find out what they really need.

One Saturday an elderly man came in very upset. His medical aid had rejected his chronic medication. He was shouting, and other customers were staring. I did not argue with him. I asked him to sit down and I brought him water. Then I asked for his card and phoned the medical aid while he waited.

It turned out his doctor had used an old code. I explained this to him in simple words. I also called the doctor's rooms to fix the script. It took about forty minutes. Before he left, the pharmacist gave him a few days of medication to cover him until Monday.

He came back the next week to thank me. That stayed with me. Most angry customers are not bad people. They are stressed, and they want someone to take them seriously.

From this I learnt three things. Stay calm, even when the customer is not. Take ownership instead of passing the problem on. And follow up, because the problem is not solved until the customer says it is. I try to use these rules every shift.
