If there was a conflict in my project team, the first thing I would do is try to understand what is actually going on before I jump in. A lot of the time people argue about the surface thing, like who is doing which task, but the real problem is something else. Maybe someone feels like their work isn't being noticed or they are stressed about marks.

So I'd talk to each person on their own first. I think its important that people feel heard and not ambushed in front of the whole group. I'd ask them what they think the problem is and what they would want to happen. I wouldn't take sides at that point, I'd just listen.

Then I would bring the group together. In second year we had a group project for Organisational Behaviour where two members basically stopped talking to each other because one of them kept changing the slides the night before we presented. It was a mess. What helped was sitting down and agreeing on a clear plan - who owns which part, and a deadline for changes. Once it was written down there was less to fight about.

I also think as a team member you have to be honest about your own part in it. Sometimes I'm the one who is too controlling, I know that about myself. So I'd try to check whether I'm making the conflict worse. My mum always says it takes two people to have an argument and she's usually right.

If it still didn't get better I would go to the lecturer or the manager, but only after we tried to sort it out ourselves. I don't want to be the person who runs to the boss every time something goes wrong. Conflict isn't always bad either. Sometimes the arguments lead to a better idea, as long as people stay respectful and focus on the work and not on each other.
