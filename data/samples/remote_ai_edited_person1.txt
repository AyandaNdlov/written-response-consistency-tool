I think conflict in a project team is pretty normal, and how you handle it makes a big difference to whether the team actually works well together. In second year we had two people in our Organisational Behaviour group who nearly stopped talking because one kept changing the slides the night before, so I've seen how quickly it can get out of hand.

Firstly, it is important to note that effective conflict resolution begins with understanding the root cause. Conflicts frequently arise from miscommunication, unclear roles, competing priorities, or differences in work styles. By taking the time to analyze the underlying issues, a team leader can ensure that the response addresses the real problem rather than its symptoms. Furthermore, active listening plays a pivotal role in this process, as it allows each team member to feel valued and understood.

Secondly, fostering open and transparent communication is essential. I would facilitate a structured discussion in which each party is given the opportunity to share their perspective without interruption. Moreover, establishing clear ground rules for respectful dialogue helps to create a psychologically safe environment and keeps the focus on shared organizational goals.

In our case what helped was sitting down and agreeing who owns which part, with a deadline for changes. Once it was written down there wasn't much left to fight about. I'd also try to be honest about my own part in it, because sometimes I'm the one being too controlling and I know that about myself.

If it still didn't get better, I'd go to the lecturer or the manager, but only after we tried to sort it out ourselves. Conflict isn't always bad - sometimes the arguments end up giving you a better idea, as long as everyone stays respectful and doesn't make it personal.
